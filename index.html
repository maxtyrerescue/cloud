Why Driving on a Flat Tyre Can Cause More Damage Discovering a flat tyre is frustrating, especially when you are close to home or only a short distance from a garage. It can be tempting to keep driving for a few minutes rather than stopping. That decision can turn a relatively simple tyre problem into a much more expensive repair. A flat tyre is not designed to support the weight of the vehicle without the correct air pressure. Once the pressure drops significantly, the tyre can no longer work as intended. The Sidewall Can Be Destroyed One of the first parts of the tyre at risk is the sidewall. Normally, air pressure helps the tyre maintain its shape and carry the vehicle's weight. When the tyre is flat, the sidewall becomes trapped and flexes excessively between the wheel and the road. Even a short distance can damage the internal structure of the tyre. A puncture that might originally have been repairable can therefore become a tyre that must be replaced. The Wheel Can Be Damaged The tyre also protects the wheel from direct contact with the road. Once the tyre loses enough pressure, the wheel can begin taking impacts that would normally be absorbed by the tyre. This can damage the rim, especially when driving over potholes, kerbs, uneven surfaces, or speed bumps. Repairing or replacing a damaged wheel can cost considerably more than fixing a simple puncture. A Repairable Puncture Can Become Unrepairable Not every puncture automatically requires a new tyre. Certain small punctures in suitable areas of the tread may be repairable after a proper inspection. However, continuing to drive while the tyre is flat creates heat, flexing, and internal damage. By the time the car reaches a garage, the original puncture may no longer be the main problem. The tyre itself may have been weakened beyond safe repair. Steering and Handling Can Become Worse A flat tyre can significantly affect how the vehicle handles. Steering may feel heavy, the car may pull to one side, and braking behaviour can become less predictable. This is particularly dangerous at higher speeds or in busy traffic. The vehicle has four relatively small contact areas with the road, and the tyres play a major role in steering, braking, and stability. Losing pressure in one of them changes how the vehicle responds. The Tyre Can Come Away From the Wheel In severe cases, driving on a completely flat tyre can cause it to separate from the wheel. The tyre may become shredded as the wheel continues rotating. Once that happens, continuing to drive becomes even more dangerous and can cause additional damage around the wheel area. At that point, what started as a small puncture may require a new tyre and potentially wheel repairs as well. Other Vehicle Components May Be Affected Driving on a flat tyre can also put unnecessary strain on surrounding components. The wheel, suspension, steering, and wheel arch area may experience impacts they would not normally receive. While major secondary damage does not happen every time, the risk increases the longer the vehicle is driven on a badly deflated tyre. What Should You Do Instead? If you suspect a tyre is flat, reduce speed carefully and stop somewhere safe as soon as possible. Avoid sudden steering or braking. Once stopped, inspect the tyre only if it is safe to do so. If you have a suitable spare wheel and know how to fit it safely, that may be an option. Otherwise, contact a mobile tyre fitting or roadside tyre assistance service. A mobile fitter can come to the vehicle and determine whether the tyre can be repaired or needs replacing. What About Run-Flat Tyres? Run-flat tyres are designed differently from standard tyres and may allow limited driving after pressure loss. However, they still have restrictions relating to distance and speed. The exact limits depend on the tyre and vehicle manufacturer. Having run-flat tyres does not mean you can continue driving indefinitely after a puncture. Check the manufacturer's guidance and arrange inspection as soon as possible. How to Reduce the Risk of a Flat Tyre You cannot prevent every puncture, but regular tyre checks can reduce the likelihood of avoidable problems. Check tyre pressures regularly and look for: cuts cracks bulges embedded nails or screws uneven wear low tread repeated pressure loss If one tyre keeps losing pressure, have it inspected rather than repeatedly topping it up. A slow leak can be an early warning of a puncture, valve problem, or wheel issue. Conclusion Driving on a flat tyre is rarely worth the risk. A small puncture may be repairable when caught early, but continuing to drive can destroy the tyre, damage the wheel, reduce vehicle control, and increase the overall repair cost. If a tyre loses significant pressure, stop somewhere safe and arrange the appropriate help. Protecting the tyre and wheel starts with one simple decision: do not keep driving when the tyre is no longer capable of supporting the car safely.
